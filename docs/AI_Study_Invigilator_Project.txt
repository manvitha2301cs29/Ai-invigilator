================================================================================
AI STUDY INVIGILATOR
A Privacy-First Deep Learning System for Study Attention, Time Management,
and Personalized Scheduling
================================================================================

Author's working title: "AI Study Invigilator" (formerly "AI Study & Attention
Analyzer"). This document is the single source of truth for the project. Use
it to brief teammates, guide AI-assisted ("vibe") coding sessions, and as the
basis for the project report / resume description.

================================================================================
1. PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================

Many students are motivated but struggle with consistency: they sit down to
study, intend to focus for a fixed block of time, and drift into distraction
or leave their desk without realizing how much time has actually been lost.
Generic advice ("take a break every 25 minutes") is not personalized, and
students have no objective record of their own focus patterns to act on.

GOAL
Build a system that:
  1. Observes a student during self-study using a laptop webcam (and,
     optionally, coarse OS activity signals).
  2. Estimates an observable, behavior-based "study engagement state"
     (NOT a mental-state or emotion diagnosis) at each moment.
  3. Lets the student set a target study block (e.g. 12:00-13:30) and
     actively monitors compliance with that block in real time.
  4. Automatically detects and times "away from desk" periods (a stopwatch
     that starts the moment the student leaves and stops when they return),
     distinct from user-declared breaks.
  5. Notifies the student live if they are away or distracted for too long,
     acting like a strict-but-fair invigilator/mentor.
  6. After each block (and over weeks of history), reports exactly how much
     time was worked properly, how much was on declared breaks, and how much
     was lost to undeclared absence/distraction.
  7. Learns the student's own sustainable focus pattern and recommends
     adjustments -- e.g. "shrink your block to 45 minutes with a mandatory
     break" or "you're consistently on-track, try extending to 105 minutes."

EXPLICIT NON-GOALS / GUARDRAILS
  - The system NEVER claims to detect emotions, mental state, or "whether
    someone is mentally focused." It only classifies observable signals:
    face orientation, gaze direction, posture, presence/absence, movement,
    and (optionally) OS activity patterns.
  - Raw webcam video is NEVER stored. Frames are processed in memory /
    on-device and discarded; only extracted numeric features and derived
    session statistics are persisted.
  - All state estimates are reported with the understanding that they are
    proxies with uncertainty, not ground truth about the student's mind.

WHY THIS MATTERS AS A PROJECT
This is not "a webcam app that checks if you're looking at the screen."
It combines computer vision, temporal deep learning, real-time systems
design (live monitoring + notifications), statistical pattern mining over
historical behavior, and an LLM reasoning layer that turns numbers into
personalized, appropriately-toned coaching. It is designed to be strong
enough for an AI/ML internship or off-campus software/ML interview
portfolio.

================================================================================
2. WHO THIS IS FOR
================================================================================

Students who are motivated but inconsistent -- they want to study, set
intentions to study for a fixed period, but lack an objective feedback loop
telling them how well they actually held to that intention. The system is
designed to function like a strict, honest mentor sitting next to them:
it doesn't nag for no reason, but when it speaks, it cites their own
measured behavior, not generic advice.

================================================================================
3. CORE FEATURES
================================================================================

A. TARGET BLOCK PLANNING
   - User sets a planned study block: start time, end time (or duration).
   - Example: "12:00 to 13:30, no-break study session."

B. LIVE ENGAGEMENT MONITORING (during an active block)
   - Face presence, head pose, gaze direction, posture, and movement are
     sampled from the webcam (~1-2 Hz) and classified into a state:
     Engaged / Idle-present / Distracted-present / Away.
   - Optional OS-activity signal (active window category + input activity
     boolean) is fused in to strengthen the "actually doing work" estimate
     -- content and keystrokes are never logged, only category + boolean.

C. AUTOMATIC AWAY STOPWATCH
   - The moment the system loses face presence beyond a short grace period
     (avoids false triggers from a head turn or bad lighting), an "Away"
     timer starts automatically.
   - The timer stops the instant the student returns.
   - This is distinct from a user-DECLARED break (student taps "Taking a
     break" before stepping away) -- undeclared absence and declared breaks
     are tracked and reported separately.

D. LIVE NOTIFICATIONS ("STRICT MENTOR" IN THE MOMENT)
   - Escalating, non-spammy nudges during an active block:
     ~30s away  -> soft nudge ("Stepped away? Mark a break or resume.")
     ~2min away -> firmer notice ("This is being logged as away time.")
   - Tone (strict / neutral / encouraging) is a configurable persona,
     applied consistently to both live notifications and end-of-block
     summaries.

E. END-OF-BLOCK REPORT
   For every completed target block, the student sees:
     - Time actually worked (Engaged + Idle-present)
     - Time on declared breaks
     - Time away/undeclared or distracted (i.e. NOT working) during the
       allocated period
     - Compliance percentage (worked time / planned duration)
     - A plain-language verdict

F. PATTERN LEARNING & SCHEDULE RECOMMENDATIONS
   - The system looks at compliance trends across the student's OWN recent
     history at a given block length (not generic population averages):
       * Consistently high compliance -> suggest extending the block
       * Consistently low compliance / frequent away-events -> suggest
         shrinking to a shorter block (e.g. 45 min) with a mandatory
         scheduled break
       * Mixed / improving trend -> suggest adding one scheduled break
         at the midpoint, keep duration the same
   - This produces a genuinely personalized "best timetable for a lazy
     but motivated student," grounded in the student's measured behavior,
     not a one-size-fits-all Pomodoro rule.

G. DASHBOARD
   - Total study time, focused time, distracted time, break count and
     duration, average uninterrupted focus stretch, focus score over time,
     daily/weekly trends, attention timeline per session, posture stats,
     and personalized recommendations.
   - Designed to be genuinely motivating: seeing "you worked 78% of your
     planned time this week, up from 61% last week" is the kind of honest,
     specific feedback that drives a motivated-but-inconsistent student to
     push harder.

H. PRIVACY BY DESIGN
   - No raw video storage, ever.
   - Processing happens locally/in-memory where possible.
   - Only numeric features and derived statistics are persisted to the
     database.

================================================================================
4. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE (LAYERED)
================================================================================

The system is built as independent, swappable layers so each can be
developed and tested in isolation before wiring them together.

  Layer 1 -- PERCEPTION
    Webcam -> Frame Capture -> Face Detection, Facial Landmarks / Head
    Pose, Eye/Gaze Features, Pose Estimation, Person Presence Detection,
    Phone/Object Detection, Second-Person Detection
    (MediaPipe Face Landmarker + Pose Landmarker + Object Detector
    [EfficientDet-Lite0, COCO-pretrained, includes a "cell phone" class];
    Second-Person Detection reuses the Face Landmarker itself, configured
    for up to 2 faces, rather than a separate model; all pretrained, not
    retrained -- this is infrastructure, not the project's contribution)

  Layer 2 -- FEATURE ENGINE
    Raw per-frame landmarks/pose -> clean numeric feature vector per
    timestep: [head_yaw, head_pitch, head_roll, gaze_x, gaze_y,
    eyes_closed_bool, face_present, posture_angle, movement_delta,
    active_window_category, input_activity_bool, ...]
    Also computes two short-window DERIVED features that Layer 3 needs to
    tell note-taking from disengagement and glances from conversations
    (see Section 6 for the full behavioral logic these support):
      - look_up_cycle_rate: how often, over the last ~30s, downward pitch
        is interrupted by a brief return to on-screen gaze (high rate =
        note-taking; near-zero rate with sustained down-pitch = a
        different, possibly concerning pattern)
      - gaze_envelope: the student's on-screen gaze zone, set once via a
        short per-user calibration step at first use, used to normalize
        gaze_x/gaze_y so wide-monitor and multi-monitor setups don't
        read as constant off-screen movement

  Layer 3 -- TEMPORAL DEEP LEARNING MODEL
    Sequence of feature vectors (a sliding window, e.g. 60-120 sec) ->
    GRU (primary, deployed model) -> Engaged / Idle-present /
    Distracted-present classification.
    ("Away" is handled as a deterministic rule directly from face-presence,
    not learned -- simpler and more accurate than asking a model to
    relearn what face detection already gives for free. Sustained
    eyes-closed beyond the configured ceiling, default 6 min, is also a
    deterministic duration rule, not a learned class -- see Section 6.)
    The model is trained to use the look_up_cycle_rate and gaze_envelope-
    normalized features specifically so it learns to separate note-taking
    and wide-monitor glancing from genuine distraction, rather than
    learning a naive "any non-forward gaze = distracted" shortcut.
    Compared against LSTM and a small Temporal Transformer encoder as an
    ablation/evaluation experiment (see Section 9).

  Layer 4 -- LIVE MONITORING & STOPWATCH ENGINE
    Consumes the classified state stream in real time during an active
    Target Block. Runs the away-detection state machine, fires live
    notifications, and lets the user declare breaks.

  Layer 5 -- SESSION ANALYTICS
    Smooths per-frame states (majority vote over a small window to remove
    jitter), splits into sessions, computes per-block summaries: worked
    time, distracted time, away time, break time, compliance %, and
    detects "unbroken work stretches" (see Section 7).

  Layer 6 -- PATTERN MINING & RECOMMENDATION ENGINE (deterministic)
    A rules/statistics engine (no LLM) over the student's historical
    TargetBlock summaries: computes trend, verdict (on_track /
    needs_shorter_blocks / needs_scheduled_breaks), and a suggested next
    schedule. This layer is fully auditable and testable on its own.

  Layer 7 -- LLM REASONING LAYER
    Takes ONLY the structured output of Layer 6 (verdict, numbers, trend)
    as input -- never raw video, never raw per-frame data -- and produces
    natural-language insights and a well-phrased recommendation in the
    configured mentor tone (strict/neutral/encouraging). Uses the Claude
    API. This is a deliberate separation: numbers come from deterministic,
    auditable code; only the phrasing comes from the LLM.

  Layer 8 -- DASHBOARD & STORAGE
    FastAPI backend, SQLite/PostgreSQL for session statistics only (never
    raw video), React/Next.js or Streamlit frontend.

FULL PIPELINE (TEXT DIAGRAM)

  Webcam
    -> Frame Capture
    -> [MediaPipe] Face Detection + Landmarks + Pose + Gaze
    -> Feature Extraction (numeric vector per timestep)
    -> Temporal DL Model (GRU) -> per-timestep state
    -> State Smoothing (majority vote)
    -> Live Monitoring Engine (Away stopwatch, notifications, break toggle)
    -> Session Analytics (stretches, sessions, per-block summaries)
    -> Recommendation Engine (deterministic trend/verdict rules)
    -> LLM Reasoning Layer (Claude API: phrasing, tone, personalized copy)
    -> Dashboard (React/Streamlit) + Database (SQLite/PostgreSQL, stats only)

================================================================================
5. STATE TAXONOMY
================================================================================

  ENGAGED             Face present, gaze on-screen, (optionally) active
                       window matches study context, recent input activity.

  IDLE-PRESENT         Face present, low movement, no recent input activity.
                       Example: reading, thinking. Deliberately NOT labeled
                       as distracted -- absence of typing isn't absence of
                       work.

  DISTRACTED-PRESENT    Face present, gaze repeatedly off-screen, or active
                       window is a non-study category.

  AWAY                Face absent beyond a short grace threshold. Handled
                       as a deterministic rule (not learned), and drives
                       the automatic stopwatch.

  FATIGUED-PATTERN (derived, session-level, not per-frame)
                       Rising idle/distracted frequency + posture decline
                       over the last N minutes RELATIVE TO THE STUDENT'S
                       OWN SESSION BASELINE. Produced by an anomaly /
                       reconstruction model (autoencoder), not a hard
                       classifier -- explicitly comparative, never an
                       absolute claim about tiredness.

================================================================================
6. FALSE-POSITIVE HANDLING -- STUDY BEHAVIOR IS NOT "ALWAYS FACING FORWARD"
================================================================================

CORE DESIGN PRINCIPLE
  No state is decided from a single frame. Every flag requires a signal to
  persist past a duration threshold that is calibrated to what real
  studying actually looks like -- not an idealized "student stares
  motionless at the screen" assumption. A single frame of head-down,
  eyes-closed, or head-turned proves nothing on its own; the pattern over
  a short window is what carries meaning. This principle is the answer to
  the most common interview question this project will get: "how do you
  avoid false positives?"

The naive version of this system (raw gaze-on-screen = focused) would
misfire constantly, because real studying includes note-taking, thinking
pauses, wide-monitor eye movement, and brief conversations that are not
distraction. Each case below is handled with its own rule, not folded
into a single threshold.

  CASE: HEAD DOWN, WRITING NOTES
    Signal pattern: head pitched down, sustained, WITH periodic brief
    glances back up toward the screen, and often paired with a steady,
    repetitive hand/wrist movement region if pose landmarks are tracked
    at that resolution.
    Handling: recognized as a distinct NOTE-TAKING pattern, not scored as
    Distracted-present. Sustained downward pitch alone is not sufficient
    to flag -- it is extremely common, legitimate study behavior and
    flagging it would make the system feel unfair almost immediately.
    Distinguishing feature vs. genuine disengagement: note-taking down-
    pitch typically shows a rhythmic look-down/look-up cycle over
    10-30 second periods; a student who has simply put their head down
    and stopped participating shows a down-pitch with no return cycle at
    all (this second pattern is closer to the eyes-closed/away logic
    below, not the note-taking case).

  CASE: HEAD TURNED TO TALK TO SOMEONE
    Signal pattern: head yaw beyond the on-screen tolerance zone, HELD
    for a sustained duration, often with detectable speech-adjacent jaw
    movement if available.
    Handling: SHOULD be flagged as Distracted-present, but only once the
    turn is sustained past a short grace period (e.g. several seconds).
    Distinguishing feature vs. a benign glance: a glance (checking a
    second monitor, looking at a phone on the desk, a quick "did someone
    call me" turn) returns to on-screen within 1-2 seconds. A
    conversation holds the turned position continuously. The duration
    threshold, not the fact of turning, is what separates these.

  CASE: WIDE / MULTI-MONITOR HORIZONTAL GAZE SWEEP
    Signal pattern: gaze_x oscillates across a wider horizontal range
    than a single laptop screen would require, but stays within a
    plausible "reading across a wide display" arc.
    Handling: NOT flagged. The on-screen gaze tolerance zone is
    calibrated per setup (see below), not a fixed universal angle. A
    student with a 34" ultrawide or dual monitors legitimately moves
    their eyes and head further left-right than someone on a 13" laptop,
    and treating that as distraction would make the system actively
    wrong for a large fraction of setups.
    Implementation note: a short one-time CALIBRATION STEP at first use
    (e.g. "look at each corner of your screen(s) for a moment") records
    the student's actual on-screen gaze envelope, and that envelope --
    not a hardcoded angle -- defines "on-screen" for that student's
    setup from then on.

  CASE: EYES CLOSED BRIEFLY WHILE THINKING
    Signal pattern: eyes closed (or near-closed) for a few seconds,
    often with head still upright and facing the screen.
    Handling: NOT flagged. This is normal cognitive behavior during
    problem-solving and reading comprehension. Treating it as distraction
    or fatigue would be exactly the kind of unsupported mental-state
    claim this project explicitly avoids (see Section 1, guardrails).

  CASE: EYES CLOSED FOR A PROLONGED PERIOD (~6+ MINUTES)
    Signal pattern: eyes closed continuously, combined with an absence
    of the normal small head/posture adjustments a working person makes,
    sustained past a long duration threshold (default: 6 minutes,
    configurable).
    Handling: SHOULD be flagged. This is different in kind from the
    brief-eyes-closed case above -- the duration is far outside the
    range of a thinking pause, and combined with stillness it is a
    meaningfully different pattern.
    Labeling discipline: the flag is worded as "prolonged eye closure --
    possible rest/sleep," an inference with stated uncertainty, NOT
    asserted as "the student is asleep." This keeps the system honest
    about the fact that it observes eye state and stillness, not
    consciousness -- a system that states behavioral inferences as
    certainties is both less accurate and easier for a critical reader
    (or interviewer) to discredit. The 6-minute default threshold should
    be justified in the report as a deliberately conservative cutoff,
    chosen to be well outside the range of normal thinking pauses (which
    the literature and common sense both place at well under a minute).

  CASE: PHONE DETECTED IN FRAME
    Signal pattern: a separate lightweight object-detection model (not
    the face/pose pipeline) run on the same webcam frame, flagging when
    a "cell phone" class is detected with reasonable confidence.
    Handling: SHOULD be flagged, gated by the same duration principle as
    everything else -- a phone that appears in-frame for a single frame
    (picked up and set back down, or briefly passing through frame) is
    not flagged; a phone held/visible for a sustained duration (e.g.
    several seconds) triggers a direct, named warning notification, e.g.
    "Don't use your phone, {name}." This is the one case in the system
    where the live notification is deliberately direct and personalized
    rather than softly worded, because unlike ambiguous gaze/posture
    signals, a phone in frame during a study block is a much less
    ambiguous signal -- there are fewer innocent explanations for it than
    for, say, a head turn.
    Model: MediaPipe Object Detector with the EfficientDet-Lite0 model,
    pretrained on the COCO dataset, which already includes "cell phone"
    as one of its 80 object classes -- this is reused, off-the-shelf
    infrastructure, not a model trained from scratch, exactly like the
    face/pose landmarkers. No custom training data is needed for this
    specific detection.
    Note on ambiguity: a phone visible on the desk but untouched (e.g.
    face-down, screen off, not being looked at) is a harder case than a
    phone held up in front of the face. A reasonable first version
    treats ANY sustained phone detection in-frame as flag-worthy and
    documents this as a known simplification; a more advanced version
    could combine phone-detection with gaze-toward-phone-region to
    reduce false positives on a phone that's simply sitting nearby.

  CASE: A SECOND PERSON ENTERS THE FRAME
    Signal pattern: the same Face Landmarker used for the primary
    student's head pose/gaze/eyes is configured to detect up to two
    faces (rather than one); a second detected face, sustained, is the
    signal.
    Handling: SHOULD be flagged, gated by the same duration principle as
    everything else -- a second face for a single frame (someone briefly
    walking past in the background, a sibling passing through the room)
    is NOT flagged; a second face present continuously for a sustained
    duration (e.g. several seconds) triggers a direct, named warning
    notification, e.g. "Stay in a quiet environment -- avoid talking to
    others, {name}." Like phone detection, this is one of the cases
    where the notification is deliberately direct rather than softly
    worded, since a second person sitting down and remaining in frame
    has fewer innocent "still studying" explanations than an ambiguous
    gaze signal.
    Model: reuses the Face Landmarker already running for the primary
    student (Layer 1) -- no separate model needed. Setting num_faces=2
    lets the same detector report whether more than one face is present;
    the system still only uses face index 0 for the primary student's
    own head pose/gaze/eyes features, and treats any additional detected
    face purely as a presence signal, not as another tracked individual.
    Labeling discipline: the flag is worded around environment/behavior
    ("stay in a quiet environment," "avoid talking to others"), not
    around a claim about what the conversation is about or who the
    second person is -- consistent with the project's guardrail against
    unsupported claims (Section 1). The system observes "a second face
    is present," not "the student is being distracted by a friend" or
    any characterization of the interaction.
    Note on ambiguity: this cannot distinguish a second person who is
    also quietly studying alongside the student (e.g. a study partner,
    a sibling doing homework at the same desk) from a second person who
    is a genuine distraction. A reasonable first version flags ANY
    sustained second-face presence and documents this as a known
    simplification, exactly as with the phone-on-desk case above; a
    future version could let a student mark a session as "co-studying"
    to suppress this specific flag for that block.

SUMMARY TABLE -- DURATION THRESHOLDS GOVERN EVERYTHING

  Behavior                          Flag?   Governing factor
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Brief head-down glance             No     Below note-taking window
  Sustained head-down w/ look-up     No     Note-taking cycle pattern
    cycle (writing notes)
  Sustained head-down, no cycle      Maybe  Falls through to distracted/
    (head just resting down)                fatigue logic if long enough
  Brief head turn (glance)           No     Under turn-duration threshold
  Sustained head turn (talking)      Yes    Over turn-duration threshold
  Wide-monitor gaze sweep            No     Inside calibrated gaze envelope
  Gaze genuinely off any screen      Yes    Outside calibrated gaze envelope,
                                            sustained
  Brief eyes-closed (thinking)       No     Under eyes-closed duration floor
  Eyes-closed 6+ minutes             Yes    Over eyes-closed duration ceiling,
                                            worded as an inference
  Phone briefly passes through frame No     Under phone-visible duration floor
  Phone held/visible, sustained      Yes    Over phone-visible duration floor;
                                            direct, personalized warning
  Second person briefly passes by    No     Under second-person duration floor
  Second person present, sustained   Yes    Over second-person duration floor;
                                            direct, personalized warning

This table is the concrete deliverable for the Feature Engine and
Temporal Model layers: every one of these rows becomes either a
duration-gated rule (cheap, deterministic, good enough for the Phase C
baseline) or a labeled pattern the temporal model is trained to
distinguish (Phase D), rather than a single-frame threshold.

================================================================================
7. KEY DERIVED METRIC: UNBROKEN WORK STRETCH
================================================================================

Definition: a maximal continuous time interval where the proportion of
Engaged + Idle-present states in a sliding window stays above a threshold,
uninterrupted by an Away period longer than a break threshold (e.g. 3 min).

Why it matters: this is the number that answers "how long has the student
worked without a break," and it is compared against the STUDENT'S OWN
historical distribution (not a generic rule) to decide whether their
current target block length is realistic. This personalization is the
project's strongest differentiator versus a generic Pomodoro app or a
plain "focus detector."

8. DATA MODEL (CORE ENTITIES)
================================================================================

TargetBlock
  id, user_id, planned_start, planned_end, planned_duration_min,
  status (scheduled/active/completed/abandoned), actual_start, actual_end,
  created_at

StateEvent (fine-grained, continuous during an active block)
  id, target_block_id, timestamp, state (engaged/idle_present/
  distracted_present/away), raw_features (yaw, pitch, gaze_xy,
  posture_angle, movement, face_conf -- kept for retraining, never raw
  video)

BreakEvent (explicit, user-declared)
  id, target_block_id, break_start, break_end, declared=true

AwayEvent (inferred, automatic -- the stopwatch)
  id, target_block_id, away_start, away_end, duration_sec, was_notified

PhoneEvent (inferred, automatic -- duration-gated, see Section 6)
  id, target_block_id, detected_start, detected_end, duration_sec,
  was_notified

SecondPersonEvent (inferred, automatic -- duration-gated, see Section 6)
  id, target_block_id, detected_start, detected_end, duration_sec,
  was_notified

TargetBlockSummary (computed once a block ends)
  target_block_id, worked_sec, distracted_sec, away_undeclared_sec,
  declared_break_sec, phone_flag_count, second_person_flag_count,
  away_event_count, compliance_pct,
  verdict (on_track/needs_shorter_blocks/needs_scheduled_breaks)

Design note: Away, Break, Phone, and Second-Person are separate tables
(not flags on one table) because each has a different trigger (automatic
vs. user-declared) and different downstream meaning -- undeclared
absence hurts compliance more than a declared, planned break, and phone/
second-person events carry their own direct, personalized notification
independent of the away-stopwatch.

================================================================================
9. DEEP LEARNING COMPONENTS
================================================================================

WHAT IS BUILT FROM SCRATCH VS. REUSED (be explicit about this in any
report or interview -- it shows engineering judgment, not just library use)

  Reused / pretrained (infrastructure, not the contribution):
    - MediaPipe Face Landmarker (468 3D landmarks)
    - MediaPipe Pose Landmarker (body landmarks for posture)
    These are not retrained -- there is no reasonable amount of data to
    beat Google's landmark models from scratch, and reinventing them adds
    no value to the project.

  Built by the student (the actual DL contribution):
    1. A lightweight head-pose / gaze regressor on top of MediaPipe
       landmarks (geometric solvePnP baseline, optionally a small MLP
       regressor -- landmark-based MLP approaches have been shown
       competitive with CNN baselines like ResNet18 for gaze estimation
       while being far cheaper to run).
    2. A temporal engagement classifier: GRU (primary/deployed) trained
       on sliding windows of the extracted feature sequence, with LSTM
       and a small Temporal Transformer encoder trained as comparison
       baselines.
    3. An autoencoder / anomaly-detection model over feature sequences to
       flag "fatigued-pattern" deviations from a student's own baseline
       (reconstruction-error based, not classification-based).
    4. The deterministic session-analytics and recommendation-rule engine
       (Layers 5-6) -- not "ML" in the modeling sense, but a real,
       designed algorithm with clear evaluation criteria.

  Explicitly not built from scratch:
    - The LLM reasoning layer uses the Claude API directly. Re-training
      or fine-tuning a language model is out of scope and would add
      nothing -- the value-add is in prompt/context design (structured
      stats in, grounded natural language out), not model training.

WHY GRU AS THE PRIMARY DEPLOYED MODEL
  - Sequences are short-to-medium (a few hundred timesteps per window at
    1-2 Hz) and the per-timestep feature vector is small (~10-15 numbers)
    -- exactly the regime where GRU's smaller parameter count tends to
    match or beat LSTM with less overfitting risk on a modestly sized
    dataset.
  - Must run continuously on a laptop CPU alongside the student's actual
    work -- GRU is cheaper at inference than a Transformer encoder.
  - Transformer is kept as an ablation/comparison model in the evaluation
    section (accuracy vs. latency vs. model size trade-off), not as the
    deployed model -- this comparison itself is a strong piece of
    evidence of rigor for a report or resume.

10. DATASET STRATEGY
================================================================================

PRIMARY PUBLIC BENCHMARK: DAiSEE
  - The standard academic benchmark for this exact problem space: 9,068
    video snippets from 112 users, labeled for engagement/boredom/
    confusion/frustration (4 ordinal levels each), collected "in the
    wild" during online lecture-watching.
  - Used to validate that the temporal architecture (GRU/LSTM/Transformer)
    works against a known, citable benchmark with real labels -- gives a
    defensible accuracy/F1 number that isn't self-labeled.
  - Known limitations to state explicitly in the report: clips are short
    (~10s), the population skews toward a specific demographic, and the
    context is online-lecture-watching, not self-directed study -- so it
    validates the ARCHITECTURE, not the exact deployment domain.

SELF-COLLECTED WEAK-LABEL DATASET (for the actual deployment domain)
  - Diary-style protocol: log webcam sessions of yourself (and
    teammates, with consent) performing scripted behaviors in sequence
    -- e.g. "look at screen and type for 2 min" (Engaged), "look away
    repeatedly" (Distracted-present), "read without typing" (Idle-
    present), "leave the desk" (Away) -- to get cheap, honest labels for
    the real use case.
  - Used to fine-tune/validate the model actually deployed, after
    architecture choice is validated on DAiSEE.
  - Responsible collection: explicit consent from anyone recorded, no
    raw video retained past feature extraction, clear ability to opt out
    and have data deleted.

12. DEPLOYMENT ARCHITECTURE -- WHY A BROWSER TAB CANNOT DO THIS ALONE
================================================================================

REQUIREMENT VS. REALITY
  Two requirements were given for this system: (1) it should work while
  the student is in a DIFFERENT tab (LeetCode, docs, etc.), and (2) it
  should be reachable by anyone via a shared link. These two requirements
  pull toward DIFFERENT architectures, and it's important to resolve this
  explicitly rather than building something that quietly breaks.

WHY A PURE WEBSITE (BROWSER TAB) CANNOT SATISFY REQUIREMENT (1)
  Browsers deliberately throttle or fully suspend JavaScript execution,
  and in many cases stop delivering camera frames, on tabs that lose
  focus or are backgrounded. This is not a bug or a missing feature to
  code around -- it is a deliberate browser power-management and privacy
  protection, present across all major browsers to varying degrees. A
  webcam-monitoring tab that is not the active, focused tab will silently
  degrade or stop working the moment the student switches to LeetCode --
  which is the exact opposite of what "study invigilator" needs to do.

THE RESOLUTION: SPLIT THE SYSTEM INTO A LOCAL WATCHER + A SHARED DASHBOARD
  - LOCAL WATCHER (does the actual monitoring):
    A small Python application, launched once at the start of a study
    block, that runs OUTSIDE the browser as its own OS process. It owns
    the webcam continuously, runs the full perception + feature +
    temporal-model pipeline, tracks the away-stopwatch, detects phones,
    and fires OS-level desktop notifications ("Don't use your phone,
    {name}") -- ALL independent of whatever tab or application currently
    has focus. This is the only architecture that guarantees monitoring
    survives the student tabbing away to work elsewhere.
    It communicates with the backend over the network (local HTTP calls
    to the FastAPI backend, or a lightweight WebSocket connection),
    sending only extracted features and state events -- never raw video.

  - SHARED WEB DASHBOARD (the "link everyone can access" part):
    A normal deployed website (FastAPI backend + React/Next.js or
    Streamlit frontend) that shows target-block planning, live status
    (reflecting what the local watcher is currently reporting), session
    history, compliance reports, and recommendations. This is what gets
    a public URL and what "anyone with the link" opens. It does NOT do
    the watching itself -- it reads and displays what the watcher has
    reported. If opened without a local watcher running, it behaves as a
    historical dashboard / planning tool rather than a live monitor.

  - HOW THEY CONNECT:
    Each user runs the local watcher on their own machine (a small
    downloadable script or packaged executable, tied to their account),
    which authenticates to the shared backend and pushes state updates.
    Multiple students can each run their own local watcher and each see
    their own data on the same shared, publicly-reachable dashboard --
    this is what makes "everyone with the link can access it" and
    "monitoring survives backgrounding" both true at once, without
    either one silently breaking the other.

WHAT THIS MEANS PRACTICALLY FOR SETUP
  A user visiting the public link for the first time needs to also
  download and run the small local watcher program once (a "Start
  Monitoring" button on the dashboard can link to this download and to
  setup instructions). This is a normal pattern for tools that need
  persistent local hardware access (screen-time trackers, some
  productivity tools work this way for the same underlying reason) and
  should be explained plainly in the product's own onboarding, not hidden.

AN ALTERNATIVE NOT PURSUED, AND WHY
  A browser extension with a persistent background service worker was
  considered as a middle-ground option. It is closer to "stays alive
  while browsing other tabs" than a plain webpage, but is still bound by
  the browser extension platform's own background-execution limits, adds
  meaningful build complexity (extension manifest, store review, per-
  browser differences), and does not remove the core tension -- it only
  narrows it. The local watcher + shared dashboard split is the more
  honest, more robust architecture and is what this project should build.

13. DEVELOPMENT PHASES / ROADMAP
================================================================================

Phases are ordered to validate cheap assumptions before expensive ones,
and to produce a working, demoable system at every milestone -- not just
at the very end.

PHASE A -- Signal feasibility check (days)
  Run MediaPipe on webcam, log raw landmarks/pose/gaze while manually
  acting "focused" / "distracted" / "away" for a few minutes each.
  Confirms the features actually separate the classes before any model
  is written.

PHASE B -- Feature extraction pipeline
  Deterministic, testable function: frame -> clean numeric feature
  vector. No DL yet. This is the foundation every later layer depends on
  -- noisy features here will look like "the model is bad" later when
  the real problem is upstream.

PHASE C -- Rule-based baseline (heuristic thresholds, no DL)
  E.g. "gaze away > 5s => distracted." Gives a working end-to-end demo
  immediately: dashboard, stretch detection, break counting, a first
  version of the Target Block monitor and stopwatch -- all running on
  simple rules. Also creates the baseline the trained model must beat.

PHASE D -- Train the temporal DL model (GRU primary, LSTM + Transformer
  as comparisons)
  Only started once features are clean (Phase B) and a baseline exists
  (Phase C) to compare against.

PHASE E -- Anomaly / fatigue-pattern model (autoencoder)
  Built after the classifier; reuses the same feature-sequence pipeline.

PHASE F -- Pattern mining + recommendation engine
  Needs multiple logged Target Blocks to be meaningful -- naturally comes
  after a few weeks of real usage data exists.

PHASE G -- LLM reasoning layer + polished dashboard + live notifications
  Wraps the now-trustworthy analytics in natural language and a
  professional UI; live notification system finalized here.

15. EVALUATION METHODOLOGY
================================================================================

MODEL-LEVEL EXPERIMENTS
  - CNN-based vs. landmark-based (geometric/MLP) features for gaze
    estimation: accuracy, latency, model size.
  - LSTM vs. GRU vs. Temporal Transformer for the engagement classifier:
    accuracy, precision, recall, F1, confusion matrix, inference latency,
    model size, CPU/GPU utilization.
  - Different temporal window sizes (e.g. 30s / 60s / 120s): effect on
    accuracy and responsiveness.
  - Different feature combinations (webcam-only vs. webcam + OS-activity
    fusion): effect on classification quality, since this directly tests
    the "gaze-at-screen isn't the same as working" hypothesis.

SYSTEM-LEVEL EVALUATION
  - Rule-based baseline vs. trained GRU model: does the DL model actually
    beat simple heuristics? (Important, honest comparison for the report.)
  - Away-detection stopwatch accuracy: false-trigger rate from head turns
    / lighting vs. true away events, measured against manually logged
    ground truth.
  - Recommendation engine validity: does the compliance-trend-based
    schedule suggestion actually track what a human reviewing the same
    logs would suggest? (Can be spot-checked manually across sessions.)

METRICS TO REPORT
  Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score, Confusion Matrix, Inference
  latency, Model size, CPU/GPU utilization.

17. LIKELY INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
================================================================================

  1. How do you know the student is "distracted" and not just thinking?
     -> Explain the Idle-present vs. Distracted-present distinction and
        why it exists.
  1b. How do you avoid flagging normal study behavior -- note-taking,
      wide-monitor eye movement, brief conversations, thinking pauses --
      as distraction?
     -> This is the strongest "how do you avoid false positives" answer
        in the project. Walk through Section 6 directly: every flag is
        duration-gated and pattern-specific, not single-frame. Note-
        taking is recognized by its look-down/look-up cycle, not just
        downward pitch. Talking is separated from a glance by sustained
        vs. brief head-turn duration. Wide-monitor movement is handled
        by a per-student calibrated gaze envelope, not a fixed angle.
        Brief eyes-closed is under a duration floor; only sustained
        eyes-closed (~6 min default) crosses into a flagged, honestly-
        worded inference ("possible rest/sleep," never asserted as fact).
  1c. What happens if a second person -- a study partner, a sibling --
      is legitimately in frame and not actually a distraction?
     -> Acknowledge this directly rather than claiming the system
        solves it perfectly: a sustained second face is flagged the
        same way a phone is, gated by duration, but the system genuinely
        cannot distinguish "co-studying" from "being distracted by
        someone" from vision alone -- it observes presence, not intent.
        State this as a documented, known simplification (Section 6),
        and mention the natural next step: letting a student mark a
        block as "co-studying" up front to suppress this specific flag.
        This kind of honest limitation, stated clearly, reads better in
        an interview than pretending the system has solved something it
        hasn't.
  2. Why GRU instead of LSTM or a Transformer?
     -> Sequence length/feature size regime, inference cost on CPU,
        ablation results.
  3. Why not just use an LLM/vision-language model for the whole thing?
     -> Cost, latency (must run continuously), privacy (no raw frames
        leaving the device), and reliability/auditability of deterministic
        stats vs. an LLM's numeric reasoning.
  4. How do you validate the recommendation engine isn't just noise?
     -> Trend-based (multiple sessions), not single-session reactive;
        explain the on_track/needs_shorter_blocks/needs_scheduled_breaks
        thresholds and why they require a pattern, not one bad session.
  5. What happens with poor lighting / no camera / glasses / multiple
     people in frame?
     -> Discuss face-detection confidence thresholds, grace periods
        before declaring "away," and out-of-scope handling.
  6. How do you avoid false "away" triggers from a simple head turn?
     -> The debounce/grace-period logic in the stopwatch state machine.
  7. How is privacy actually enforced, not just claimed?
     -> No raw frame persistence, only numeric features/statistics
        written to disk; explain exactly what is and isn't stored.
  8. What's your model's actual accuracy, and how does it compare to a
     simple rule-based baseline?
     -> Be ready with real numbers from Phase C vs. Phase D comparison.
  9. How would this generalize to a different student than the ones in
     your training/validation data?
     -> Discuss DAiSEE's demographic limitations, the value of the
        self-collected diary dataset, and per-student baseline comparison
        for the fatigue/anomaly model (compares a student to their own
        history, not to a population).
  9b. Why isn't this just a website -- why does it need a local
      component at all?
     -> Explain the browser tab-backgrounding constraint directly
        (Section 12): browsers throttle or suspend JS execution and
        camera access on unfocused tabs, which is fundamentally
        incompatible with "monitor continuously while the student works
        in a different tab." Walk through the local-watcher +
        shared-dashboard split and why it's the honest solution rather
        than something that looks simpler but silently breaks.
  10. How would you scale this to many students (e.g. a classroom
      product)?
      -> On-device inference keeps per-user compute local; backend only
         stores aggregated statistics; discuss what would need to change
         (auth, multi-tenant DB, aggregate-only analytics dashboards for
         a teacher view) versus what stays the same.
